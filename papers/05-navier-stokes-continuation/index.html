Abstract

The nonlinear-calculus matrix-of-error framework leads, in its three-dimensional Navier–Stokes application, to a continuation coefficient proportional to ν−3∥Pχω∥L24\nu^{-3}\|P_\chi\omega\|_{L^2}^4, where PχP_\chi retains two fixed vorticity components. This note recalls the identification, made in [1], of the condition with the spatial-exponent-two case of the Chae–Choe criterion, explains its origin in the stretching estimate, and compares its unforced use with the smoothly forced breakdown reported by OpenAI.

Three statements organize the comparison. First, interpolation and Young's inequality produce a scale-critical continuation integral that is not controlled by the usual energy bound alone. Second, smooth forcing adds a finite inhomogeneous contribution to the enstrophy envelope without changing the functional form of the blockwise stretching coefficient; it nevertheless changes the evolving field and can change the coefficient's accumulated value. Third, accepting the reported periodic breakdown theorem, the selected two-component integral must diverge at its finite singular horizon. The continuation criterion and that construction are therefore compatible: the construction lies outside the unforced input class and fails the sufficient condition in the enlarged forced class. Exact finite examples and a qualified core-scaling comparison make this relationship explicit. The note does not settle class-wide finiteness for unforced data.

Overview

This expository note examines the two-component vorticity control selected in the earlier Matrix-of-Error analysis of three-dimensional Navier-Stokes. It recalls that the resulting continuation condition is the spatial-exponent-two case of the established Chae-Choe criterion and explains its appearance through the stretching estimate, interpolation, and Young’s inequality. The selected critical integral is not bounded by the usual energy estimate alone. The note then extends the enstrophy envelope to smooth forcing: forcing adds an inhomogeneous contribution while retaining the functional form of the stretching coefficient, although it changes the evolving field and its accumulated control. Accepting OpenAI’s reported periodic forced-breakdown theorem as a premise, the note shows that this integral must diverge at the singular horizon. Finite examples and a qualified scaling comparison explain the compatibility. The analysis does not establish class-wide finiteness for unforced initial data.
