Abstract

This note makes explicit an interpretation of relativity that motivated the author’s matrix-of-error framework of nonlinear calculus. The interpretation emphasizes space and time as one physical structure and sets out two linked interpretations: an intrinsic density of space affects relativistic behavior, and time exists because of space. Gravitational light-travel delay is its central propagation example: the proposed density supplies a physical reading of metric-dependent travel times while light remains locally measured at c. The time–space statement is an explanation of why relativistic relationships should hold, not a separate new effect established here. The extension connects the resulting matrix-of-error work back to established relativity rather than proposing a replacement theory.

The note connects this account to the shared derivation-and-testing process reported in the preceding nonlinear-calculus paper. That process represented physical corrections as retained matrix contributions and recovered established results across distinct dynamical systems, including three-dimensional Navier–Stokes. Its mathematical results supply a concrete outcome of the interpretation’s methodological use, without establishing the interpretation’s proposed physical mechanism. The companion density study specifies a dust-plus-vacuum stress and calculates a linear density-to-metric-to-light-delay chain. Those calculations give the density interpretation a concrete candidate realization: the dust-like component can represent a dark-matter contribution, but its calculated response does not distinguish an intrinsic-space interpretation from other sources with the same stress and dynamics. Neither central interpretation is proved by that agreement. The contribution is to connect conceptual motivation, established relativistic mathematics, and the completed calculations without identifying them as the same result. Broader applications are anticipated but are not defined as a new problem class here.

Overview

This interpretation note explains the physical motivation behind the author’s Matrix-of-Error framework. It proposes that space has an intrinsic density relevant to relativistic behavior and that time exists because of space, while maintaining established relativity as the mathematical starting point. Gravitational light-travel delay supplies the central example: metric-dependent clock and travel-time relationships are interpreted through the proposed spatial contribution without changing locally measured light speed. The note connects this motivation to the preceding cross-system derivations and numerical tests, whose mathematical outcomes do not independently establish the proposed physical mechanism. It also incorporates the companion density study’s dust-plus-vacuum candidate and linear density-to-metric-to-delay calculation. The dust-like term can represent a dark-matter contribution, but identical stress and dynamics permit other physical origins. The contribution is the explicit relationship between interpretation, established relativistic mathematics, and completed calculations; neither the intrinsic-density claim nor the time-space explanation is proved by that relationship.
