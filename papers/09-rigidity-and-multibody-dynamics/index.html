Abstract

This sequel to Nonlinear Calculus in Practice develops mathematical connections and worked applications of the Matrix-of-Error framework. Lebesgue integration provides the common foundation linking the earlier probability and quantum-measurement studies to time-dependent budgets, deformation measures, and interacting-body mechanics. A general weighted-budget lemma, abstracted from the earlier enstrophy envelope, supplies equality and quantitative control, with a finite threshold under a persistent decay margin; the periodic flow identifies its rigid configuration. The connection to measured group theory is examined through the work of Margulis, Bader, and Furman, while geometric rigidity supplies a separate, direct connection to solid deformation. An explicit volume-preserving warping map supplies a worked geometric integration. A separate proposed test model couples solid motion, internal strain, and heat through seven stress blocks and companion evolution balances. Worked gravitational and articulated five-body examples retain pair interactions, configuration-dependent inertia, and applied contributions through integration by parts. A controlled comparison finds that replacing resolved bodies by point centers can shift fitted masses by up to 3.205% without improving wider-range held-out gravity predictions. An additional quadrupole correction reduces that mass error to 0.236%; a separate in-range test distinguishes local transfer from extrapolation. Retaining the full volume contribution recovers the known parameters near the observed numerical resolution. A separate leading-quadrupole calculation accounts coherently for radiation from all five bodies. The result is a continuation of the Matrix-of-Error integration process through further mathematical systems, with analytic identities, numerical consistency checks, and prospective physical applications distinguished explicitly.

Overview

This sequel applies the Matrix-of-Error process to mathematical connections and interacting physical systems, with Lebesgue integration as their shared foundation. It carries the earlier binary and quantum-measurement correspondence into weighted dynamical budgets, equality conditions, quantitative rigidity, and the Navier–Stokes enstrophy envelope. Measured-group-theory connections are examined separately from geometric rigidity and a worked volume-preserving deformation. A proposed solid model adds internal strain, heat, and seven stress blocks with companion balances. Gravitational and articulated multibody examples retain pair interactions, configuration-dependent inertia, applied forces, and their signed integrated contributions. The central comparison holds the integration and fitting process fixed while varying the gravity representation: retaining the full resolved-volume contribution recovers known parameters near the observed numerical resolution, while reduced representations reveal the consequences of omitted structure. A separate coherent quadrupole calculation includes all five bodies in the radiation signal. Analytical identities, numerical checks, proposed models, and future physical applications remain explicitly distinguished.
