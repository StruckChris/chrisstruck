Abstract summary

This paper proposes the Universal Matrix-of-Error Theory as a common derivation-and-testing process for dynamical systems, supplied with each system’s variables and established mathematical inputs. It applies one weak-form procedure to Mercury’s perihelion dynamics, viscous Burgers dynamics, and two- and three-dimensional Navier-Stokes vorticity, then derives a complete three-dimensional stretching-and-diffusion representation. Numerical comparisons test decomposition, rotational robustness, diffusion blocks, enstrophy balance, and amplitude-scaled flow regimes against an earlier incomplete form. A final analytical section connects the exact enstrophy identity to a selected two-component majorant and a conditional smooth-continuation criterion, with comparisons to Chae-Choe and Miller. The manuscript presents universal smooth continuation as the theory’s interpretation and identifies the exact identities, proved conditional implication, and completed finite numerical observations supporting that interpretation. It establishes the series’ cross-system Matrix-of-Error construction and the fluid example developed further in subsequent papers.
