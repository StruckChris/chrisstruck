Abstract

The Bare Binary Biconditional selected 121/2 as the unique exchange-invariant law on a bare binary alternative and connected independent repetitions of that law to half-frequency and Lebesgue measure. This paper develops the separate dynamic question that originally motivated the biconditional and the present research series: in what precise sense can an accumulating observation record approach a definite binary outcome? A self-contained classical result is given first. Let Θ∈{0,1}\Theta\in\{0,1\} have the symmetric prior, let observations be conditionally independent with strictly positive and distinguishable likelihoods, and let qn=P(Θ=1∣Y1,…,Yn)q_n=P(\Theta=1\mid Y_1,\ldots,Y_n). Then (qn)(q_n) is a bounded martingale beginning at 121/2, and identifiability, not martingale convergence alone, gives qn→1{Θ=1}q_n\to\mathbf1_{\{\Theta=1\}} almost surely. Its two endpoints therefore have equal probability. A binary-probe specialization places the observation record itself in {0,1}N\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}; a Gaussian model is retained as a continuous-observation comparison. Repeated independent runs, rather than the dependent observations within one run, produce the fair binary endpoint record connected to [1]. Bauer and Bernard's repeated quantum non-demolition measurement theorem supplies a bounded quantum counterpart: pointer weights are martingales; under their nondegeneracy condition they converge to pointer-state endpoints; the endpoint probabilities are the initial pointer weights; and relative entropy governs the asymptotic decay of losing weights. Their theorem does not choose 121/2. This paper imports that starting value only under the explicit bare symmetric premise of [1]. Reproducible classical simulations illustrate finite-depth posterior behavior and information-rate scaling without simulating quantum dynamics or treating finite posteriors as completed limits. The remaining claim that observation gives physical significance to this convergence structure is stated separately as the author's quantum convergence conjecture.

Overview

This paper investigates how an accumulating observation record can identify a definite binary outcome. In a classical model with a symmetric prior and conditionally independent observations having positive, distinguishable likelihoods, the conditional probability is a bounded martingale. Identifiability establishes convergence to the true binary endpoint, with equal endpoint probabilities inherited from the initial symmetry. Binary-probe and Gaussian examples illustrate finite-record posterior behavior and information-rate scaling. Independent repetitions of complete runs produce the fair endpoint records connected to the Bare Binary Biconditional; observations within one run remain a different object. The paper then uses Bauer and Bernard’s repeated quantum nondemolition measurement theorem as a quantum counterpart: under its nondegeneracy condition, pointer weights converge to endpoints whose probabilities are their initial weights. The value one half enters through the explicit symmetric premise. The simulations are classical comparisons, while the broader physical interpretation of observation and convergence remains the author’s separately stated quantum convergence conjecture.
