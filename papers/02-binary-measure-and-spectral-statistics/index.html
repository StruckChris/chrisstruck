Abstract summary

This computational companion develops three finite studies related to the Bare Binary Biconditional while preserving their different mathematical roles. The first maps 400,000 binary records of depth 40 to the unit interval, comparing fair sampling with a biased law. It illustrates the exact dyadic measure correspondence and the finite-resolution appearance of a biased measure. The second enumerates Liouville signs, nonzero Möbius signs, and odd-prime residue classes through an arithmetic cutoff of three million. Their observed frequencies lie near one half, but their counts, ties, and sign-change patterns differ. The third compares statistics of cached zeta zeros with established Montgomery–Odlyzko benchmarks. Reproducible definitions, inputs, and computational procedures support the comparisons. The paper distinguishes direct illustrations of an analytic identity from arithmetic and spectral context, without treating deterministic records as independent random trials or extracting asymptotic conclusions from finite data.
