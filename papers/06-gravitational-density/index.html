Abstract

This paper compares three descriptions of gravitationally relevant density: a dilute population of massive Yang–Mills-inspired states, the matter-like contribution in mimetic gravity, and vacuum stress. The motivating question is whether a positive excitation mass scale, an effective density expressed through gravitational variables, and a vacuum contribution can support a common description of an intrinsic gravitational contribution of space. The comparison is organized by the comoving energy balance d(Jρ)dt=−pJ˙+JQ\mathrm d(J\rho)/\mathrm dt=-p\dot J+JQ, with pressure and exchange retained explicitly.

The calculations identify a conditional correspondence. Conserved massive populations in a pressureless description have density ∑imiNiJ\sum_i m_iN_i/J, while mimetic matter has density CJC/J; matching the coefficient and four-velocity matches their effective dust stress tensors. A two-species finite-temperature calculation quantifies the approach to this description rather than imposing zero pressure. Separately conserved vacuum stress instead has p=−ρp=-\rho and constant density. Assigning it a matter-like density history requires a nonzero exchange term, calculated and independently integrated here.

The contribution is a comparative exposition: matching density is distinguished from matching stress and from identifying physical origin. Numerical checks verify the implementation of established formulas and illustrate their consequences; they are not independent evidence for an intrinsic density of space. The comparison does not construct a Yang–Mills mass gap. It specifies why the proposed identification of dark matter with space itself requires more than a common mass scale or homogeneous density history.

A focused extension completes a candidate dust-plus-vacuum stress with its gravitational coupling and conservation laws. A linear Fourier mode on two coupled backgrounds connects density evolution to the metric and to a clock-referenced light-travel delay. The mixed example's evolving potential gives a delay approximately 0.775%0.775\% below its frozen-potential reference. Under matched dynamics and initial data, this candidate reproduces the corresponding dust gravitational response without changing locally measured light speed; that effective agreement does not select the physical origin of its density.

Overview

This paper compares a dilute population of massive Yang-Mills-inspired states, mimetic matter, and vacuum stress using a common comoving energy balance that retains pressure and exchange. It shows the conditional stress-tensor correspondence between conserved pressureless massive populations and mimetic dust when their coefficients and four-velocities match. A two-species finite-temperature calculation quantifies the approach to the pressureless description. Separately conserved vacuum stress instead maintains constant density, and imposing a matter-like density history requires a calculated exchange term. The paper distinguishes matching a density, matching a stress tensor, and identifying a physical origin; supplied masses do not construct a Yang-Mills mass gap. A focused extension specifies a dust-plus-vacuum candidate with gravitational coupling and conservation, then follows a linear Fourier mode through density evolution, metric response, and clock-referenced light-travel delay. The resulting effective agreement illustrates the candidate’s consequences without identifying an intrinsic density of space as their unique origin.
