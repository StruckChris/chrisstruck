Abstract

This paper synthesizes established results connecting bare binary symmetry, half-probability, asymptotic half-frequency, Lebesgue measure, integration, and symmetric binary quantum measurement. Zurek's envariance program seeks to explain the equal-weight assignment through physical symmetry, while published critiques isolate the transition from state symmetry to probability symmetry as the disputed step. A probability law νp\nu_p on {0,1}\{0,1\} is called bare here when exchanging the labels 0 and 1 leaves the law invariant. If τ(0)=1\tau(0)=1 and τ(1)=0\tau(1)=0, then

τ∗νp=νp⟺p=12.\tau_*\nu_p=\nu_p \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p=\frac12.

Independent identical repetition gives the Bernoulli product measure μp=νp⊗N\mu_p=\nu_p^{\otimes\mathbb N} on Ω={0,1}N\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}. Under the dyadic map

ϕ(ω)=∑k=1∞ωk2−k,\phi(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\omega_k2^{-k},

its pushforward is Lebesgue measure λ\lambda on [0,1][0,1] exactly when p=12p=1/2. Writing

Sn(ω)=∑k=1nωk,S_n(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\omega_k,

the complete correspondence within the Bernoulli family is

τ∗νp=νp⟺p=12⟺ϕ∗μp=λ⟺μp({ω:limn→∞Sn(ω)n=12})=1.\tau_*\nu_p=\nu_p \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p=\frac12 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \phi_*\mu_p=\lambda \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mu_p\!\left( \left\{\omega: \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{S_n(\omega)}{n}=\frac12 \right\} \right)=1.

The final condition entails that both 0 and 1 occur infinitely often. Informally, and with the bare and independent-repetition qualifications made explicit: 0 and 1 states imply the 121/2 law, while convergence to 121/2 implies that both states occur without bound.

The pushforward identity links the binary system directly to calculus because Lebesgue integration over [0,1][0,1] is integration over fair infinite binary records in a second representation.

Existing binary quantum measurements already supply the physical two-outcome behavior and recorded sequences used in this synthesis; no additional measurement is proposed or required. Everett relates repeated measurement records to product-measure and frequency reasoning. For the paper's bounded physical-bareness case, Zurek supplies exchange symmetry, Bell excludes local predetermined accounts of the relevant correlations, Pironio and collaborators certify genuine randomness under stated assumptions, and Colbeck and Renner limit improved prediction under theirs. These results contribute different parts of the physical case; none alone selects 121/2. The explicit bareness premise supplies that selection: within the bounded physical description, a structureless binary alternative is represented by an exchange-invariant probability law.

For a specified symmetric state and binary measurement, the Gleason-Busch trace rule also yields equal probabilities. The present synthesis gives an independent route: under the explicit bareness premise, the bounded physical case is represented by a swap-invariant binary law, which uniquely fixes 121/2, and that same value uniquely produces Lebesgue measure and the continuum used by integration. This is a symmetric binary account, not a general derivation of unequal quantum weights. The author's conjecture about observation remains a separate proposed research direction.

Overview

This paper brings together established results connecting binary symmetry, probability, infinite records, Lebesgue measure, integration, and symmetric quantum measurement. A binary probability law is called bare when exchanging its two outcome labels leaves the law unchanged. That symmetry fixes both probabilities at one half. With independent identical repetition, the same fair law produces almost-sure half-frequency and becomes Lebesgue measure on the unit interval under binary expansion. The resulting measure identity connects integration over the interval with expectation over fair infinite binary records. The paper then examines how this structure relates to the equal-weight question in envariance arguments. Its physical application uses an explicit bareness premise, supported by a synthesis of foundational literature rather than inferred from a two-outcome label alone. The result concerns symmetric binary probabilities; a proposed interpretation of observation is retained separately as a conjecture.
